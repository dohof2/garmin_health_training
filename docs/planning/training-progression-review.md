Availability and weekly budgets are ceilings, not prescriptions. Derive an initial long-session dose from recent completed cycling durations/consistency and available feedback, using conservative transparent app heuristics; never prescribe five hours merely because Friday allows it. Progress only with evidence of tolerated work; insufficient history uses a conservative disclosed starting dose, not invented fitness. Current approved upcoming excessive sessions must be reviewed with downward duration adjustments and rationale while preserving goals, events and history.

Prefer short easy cycling on available cycling days when compatible with load/recovery and user intent. True rest remains appropriate, especially pain/illness or severe fatigue. Readiness is one contextual signal alongside recorded load, recent hard/long sessions, feedback and symptoms. Missing readiness is unknown, not zero; a moderately low score alone must not universally cancel training. Preserve all safety holds and avoid invented future health. A stale 225 W FTP remains a dated reference, not fresh calibration or a mandate to sustain135–169 W for hours.

Scientific rationale: the [IOC load/injury consensus](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096581) supports individualized load management incorporating external/internal stress and athlete wellbeing. The [IOC load/illness consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5013087/) describes individual variability and combining training diaries, load and recovery observation. The [load monitoring consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/) provides a framework rather than a single readiness cutoff. Implementation thresholds are conservative product heuristics, not universal validated percentage rules or guarantees of improved FTP/fitness.
